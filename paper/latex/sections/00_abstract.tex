\begin{abstract}
Compact edge Transformers offer a setting in which token retention, operand
precision, and execution depth can be restricted to a small set of
hardware-regular modes. This paper studies EAT-FPGA Lite, a four-block encoder
with feature width 128, four attention heads, and feed-forward expansion two,
for human activity recognition and speech keyword classification. Quality,
Balanced, and Energy-Saver modes combine eight-bit or four-bit weights,
chronological token-group retention, residual token summaries, and a block-two
exit boundary. The current prototype connects a shared tensor engine, tiled
online attention, fixed-point nonlinear operators, and a host-controlled VHDL
peripheral. Preliminary software evaluation over three training seeds yields
Quality-mode test accuracies of 93.474 percent for human activity recognition
and 92.328 percent for speech. An aligned PyTorch model matches a separate
integer reference exactly for the verified model families, and selected RTL
simulations also match their golden outputs. These results provide a numerical
baseline for hardware evaluation. Post-route feasibility, board latency, and
whole-board power and energy measurements remain pending. The final study
must establish attributable benefits through same-board baselines and
controlled precision, dropping, summary, and depth ablations.
\end{abstract}
